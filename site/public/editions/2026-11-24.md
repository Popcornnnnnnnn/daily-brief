## Today in 90 seconds

1. **Upload2Shell turns ordinary document and image pipelines into a patch-now problem.** A Ghostscript Type 1 font use-after-free can bypass `-dSAFER` and reach command execution through systems that call Ghostscript indirectly, including upload previews, thumbnails, printing and email rendering. ## Tech & AI

### Upload2Shell: Ghostscript escapes the sandbox through a font loader

PwnAI’s November 21 research describes **CVE-2026-125396**, a use-after-free in Ghostscript’s Type 1 font loader that can escape the PostScript `-dSAFER` sandbox and execute operating-system commands. The practical risk is broader than “someone opened a PostScript file”: Ghostscript often sits behind ImageMagick, Pillow, CUPS, web upload previews, thumbnailers and document converters. The researchers published a proof of concept and documented product-specific paths affecting systems including Debian CUPS, Roundcube, Plesk and WordPress.

Sophos issued its advisory on **November 23**, inside this edition’s window, and said it had no evidence of exploitation in the wild at publication time. The root fix is in **Ghostscript 10.08.0**, but downstream vendors may backport it under older-looking package versions. The useful action is therefore to inventory every service that accepts or renders untrusted documents or images, trace whether it invokes Ghostscript directly or indirectly, and verify the vendor’s fixed package—not merely assume `-dSAFER` is sufficient.

[Upload2Shell research](https://pwn.ai/upload2shell/research.html) · [Sophos advisory](https://www.sophos.com/en-us/security-advisories/sophos-sa-20261123-upload2shell)

> **Source:** [pwn.ai](https://pwn.ai/upload2shell)

### Catch-up: MiniMax’s internal model attacks cryptographic constructions

On **November 20**, MiniMax said an internal-only model called **MIM** had automated practical cryptanalysis work across historical NIST competition candidates and published schemes. Its report covers executable attacks or implementation findings involving examples such as Waterfall-256, Blender-256, LOCKER, ROLLO, RQC/LAC, DRS, COLM, SBC-VOLE and ARADI. The supporting repository includes code and write-ups, which makes the claims inspectable rather than purely promotional.

Two cautions matter. First, the model itself has not been released, so outsiders cannot reproduce the full agentic workflow. Second, the repository received a **November 23 correction for the Blender issue**. That does not invalidate the broader work, but it makes the right standard clear: treat the report as a vendor claim backed by public artifacts, then wait for independent cryptographers to reproduce each result.

[MiniMax report](https://www.minimax.io/blog/practical-cryptanalysis-with-minimaxs-latest-internal-model) · [Research repository](https://github.com/MiniMax-AI/Research) · [November 23 correction](https://github.com/MiniMax-AI/Research/commit/640514fdde70360e80995211ed4f19e586e81f0e)

> **Source:** [www.minimax.io](https://www.minimax.io/blog/practical-cryptanalysis-with-minimaxs-latest-internal-model)

### Important brief: Google SecOps delayed normalization in Singapore

Google reported a **Google SecOps incident in** `asia-southeast1` from November 23, 15:00 to 21:30 China Standard Time. A surge in log-processing load created a normalization backlog, delaying new data, searches and dashboards. Google said logs were safely queued, no data was lost, and no customer action was required. The incident is resolved, but teams that depend on near-real-time detections should distinguish delayed visibility from missing telemetry when reconstructing alerts from that period.

[Official incident report](https://status.cloud.google.com/security/incidents/MPYFjWBhRBLNUxN4KPmX)

## Open source & developer tools

### Recent hot project — os6/nospeaker turns a trackpad into a tiny speaker

**Actual release date: November 22. Why it is included now:** the project entered the collector’s new-repository feed and showed measurable, time-bounded attention rather than merely a large lifetime star count. It had **413 stars in the November 23 08:20 CST snapshot and 1,299 at the cutoff check**, an increase of 886 in roughly twenty hours.

`nospeaker` is a one-file Swift experiment with no dependencies. It drives a Force Touch trackpad’s haptic motor at frequencies up to 4 kHz, producing robotic speech and simple music without using the Mac’s audio speakers. The v1.0 release is signed and notarized, and the project publishes a ZIP digest.

The constraints are substantial: it requires a Force Touch trackpad; the author reports only limited hardware testing; and it relies on Apple’s private, undocumented MultitouchSupport interface, so a macOS update may break it. There is a one-line installer, but reviewing the script and release artifact is safer than piping an unfamiliar URL directly into a shell.

### Worth researching — Adhishtanaka/thir, a pane-based Rust environment

**Actual release date: November 23, 02:39 CST for v0.7.1. Why it is included now:** it is a recent, hands-on systems experiment surfaced by project and community discovery, not a claim that it is a top-ranked repository.

`thir` boots a Linux 6.12 kernel and then uses an all-Rust userspace. Its desktop is organized around panes, with a shell and small applications; optional BYOK AI commands begin with `!` and show a preview before execution. The repository provides VM images for VirtualBox and QEMU as well as a path for real hardware.

This is still an experimental build. The recommended development path needs macOS, Docker and QEMU; the first WebKit build can take hours; and writing the image with `dd` can destroy the wrong disk if the target is misidentified. The current repository has little cumulative attention, so “worth researching” is more accurate than “hot.”

## Business opportunity

### A read-only Upload2Shell exposure mapper for small software teams

**Observed need:** the Ghostscript flaw reaches applications through indirect document and image dependencies, while distribution backports make simple version checks unreliable. **Business hypothesis:** a small, read-only tool could scan repositories, container images and SBOMs for Ghostscript/ImageMagick/Pillow/CUPS paths, map which public upload or preview features can reach them, and produce a vendor-specific remediation report.

A low-cost validation would be to interview five small SaaS teams that accept PDFs or images, then manually produce one exposure map from an exported SBOM and architecture sketch. The main risk is false confidence: a dependency scanner cannot prove that every runtime path is safe, and vendor-specific backports can create both false positives and false negatives. The product should report evidence and uncertainty, not issue a binary “secure” badge.

[Underlying research, November 21](https://pwn.ai/upload2shell/research.html) · [Sophos advisory, November 23](https://www.sophos.com/en-us/security-advisories/sophos-sa-20261123-upload2shell)
