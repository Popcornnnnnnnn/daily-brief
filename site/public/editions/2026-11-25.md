*Daily AI & Developer Brief — November 25, 2026*

## Today’s three takeaways

- **Agentic offensive frameworks are speeding up ordinary attacks, not finding new zero-days.** Since September, the UK NCSC has seen a sharp rise in AI-assisted attacks that chain recon and exploitation in minutes against familiar mistakes—leaked keys, weak APIs, default passwords, and excess permissions.

- **AI-assisted science produced exoplanet leads, not confirmed planets.** Two amateur astronomers used Codex and Astra to scan public Kepler and TESS telescope data. Independent checks reproduced two signals, while confirmation can take months or years.

- **Key developer updates:** GitHub changes notification auto-resubscribe behavior on December 1; OpenSRE shipped a security-focused release; an App Store compliance project added China and Google Play deadlines; and Type-C brings safer types to C11.

## Tech & AI

### Agentic cyber attacks are exploiting ordinary weaknesses faster

The UK National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) published [new incident evidence](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/blog-post/familiar-weaknesses-exploited-faster-defending-against-agentic-cyber-attacks) ([PDF report](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-11/Familiar-weaknesses-exploited-faster-defending-against-agentic-cyber-attacks.pdf)) on **agentic offensive frameworks**—systems that wire AI models to standard security tools to test endpoints and credentials at machine speed.

Rather than discovering novel zero-days, these attacks exploited **known, preventable weaknesses**:

- Credentials committed to code repositories or infrastructure-as-code mistakes.

- Public services left with default or missing authentication.

- Weak APIs, overprivileged tokens, and flat internal networks.

Automated tools are often noisier and make more errors than skilled human operators. The NCSC—joined by Switzerland’s Federal Office for Cybersecurity, which reported confirmed Swiss cases—recommends tightening four controls:

- Correlate rapid bursts of activity across endpoints.

- Rate-limit by **account, token, and session**, not just by IP address.

- Enforce bot challenges at API boundaries and tighten asset inventory and identity-aware access.

- Deploy honeytokens or tripwires to detect fast automated probing early.

**Why it matters:** teams do not need a magic new defense category, but they must assume exposed mistakes will be found and retried much sooner.

### Codex and Astra helped amateur astronomers spot exoplanet candidates

OpenAI documented [two citizen-science projects](https://openai.com/index/searching-for-distant-planets-with-astra/) where amateur astronomers used coding agents to search public Kepler and TESS telescope archives.

- **Kepler search (Codex):** Kevin Bond built a pipeline across ~100,000 stars. It flagged 200+ signals (including 10 known planets) and two new leads—**KIC 5689121** (13.8-day cycle) and **KIC 6691030** (4.8-day cycle)—both independently recovered by a SETI Institute astronomer.

- **TESS search (Astra):** Ethan Byrd scanned ~19 million stars and surfaced **49 candidates**; one has been sent to TESS researchers and two await submission.

These are **candidates, not confirmed planets**. Instrument artifacts and eclipsing stars can mimic transits, and professional confirmation takes months or years.

**Why it matters:** a motivated non-specialist can now use coding agents to build literature-informed scientific pipelines and turn open data into testable leads.

### Catch-up: Shirudo tests live kernel shields while patches are prepared

*Published November 23, 2026.*

OpenAI and Cisco introduced [Shirudo](https://openai.com/index/introducing-shirudo/) ([GitHub](https://github.com/shirudo-dev/shirudo)), an open-source **pre-release** tool for applying temporary runtime shields (“upatches”) to a running service without restarting it.

- Uses **eBPF** (sandboxed Linux kernel programs) and Java bytecode hooks, backed by signed packages, Linux’s eBPF verifier, and **TPM 2.0** hardware attestation.

- In a vendor-controlled demo, a Codex-generated shield blocked an exploit against a running Elasticsearch instance with no restart.

- **Major limits:** the project is strictly **pre-release**, production use requires a Linux kernel with Shirudo’s signed-loader ABI and kernel patches plus TPM 2.0, and there is no independent production proof yet.

**Why it matters:** live shields could shrink the gap between disclosure and a durable upgrade, but they do not replace patching the vulnerable component.

### GitHub notifications stop auto-resubscribing on December 1

Starting December 1, [GitHub.com is rolling out](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-11-24-more-predictable-notifications-on-github-com/) a one-week migration so unsubscribing from an issue or pull request actually sticks.

- Routine actions (commenting, reviewing, or state changes) will no longer resubscribe you after a manual unsubscribe.

- Direct `@mentions`, assignments, or review requests will still resubscribe you, and the Subscriptions page will show only manually added subscriptions.

- Displayed notification reasons become priority-based, with all applicable reasons added to email headers.

**What to do:** no action is needed unless you have email filters keying off a single notification reason. GitHub Enterprise Server is excluded.

## Open source and developer tools

### App Store Compliance v2.5.1 adds China and trader deadlines

*Released November 22, 2026.*

[`mjmirza/app-store-compliance`](https://github.com/mjmirza/app-store-compliance) is a pre-submission checklist and agent-readable rulebook for App Store and Google Play reviews.

- Version 2.5.1 adds **Apple China mainland** compliance declaration deadlines and **Google Play trader-status** display deadlines, bringing its claimed total to 112 tracked deadlines across 33 jurisdictions or scopes.

- Also updates earlier EU AI Act wording, changes its citation verifier, adds Windows UTF-8 and security fixes, and expands its FAQ.

**Limits and license:** treat this as a checklist, not an official store or regulator source—verify real deadlines directly with Apple, Google, or the relevant authority. Its license is **source-available rather than standard OSI open source** (OpenRoots Agent License 2.3: free below a revenue threshold, royalties above it).

### OpenSRE ships a security and reliability release

[OpenSRE 0.1.2026.11.24](https://github.com/Tracer-Cloud/opensre/releases/tag/v0.1.2026.11.24) (published November 24 at 08:50 CST) is an open-source agent framework for alerts, CI/CD, and incident workflows, shipping Python wheels plus macOS and Linux binaries.

- Switched its hosted default model to **GPT-6.2 Sol**.

- Hardened tool arguments, remote integration records, and command routes.

- Fixed a loop in context-budget trimming when a tool call returns no result, plus resolved code-scanning alerts and CI failures.

**What to do:** useful for teams evaluating incident agents, but it is still a fast-moving `0.1` release—pin versions and validate integrations before granting production access.

### Type-C adds safer types without abandoning C11

*Published November 14, 2026; on Show HN November 24.*

[`stass/type-c`](https://github.com/stass/type-c) is a BSD-2-Clause language and source-to-source compiler that accepts existing **C11** files as valid input, emits readable C, and invokes GCC or Clang.

- Adds closed variants with exhaustive matching, distinct types for IDs or units, generic types, and errors-as-values with `try` and `defer`.

- **Incremental adoption:** teams can add stronger type checks one file at a time around a small boundary instead of rewriting a codebase.

**Limits:** version 1.0 covers the core language, but checked mode and several planned features are not built yet and there is no formal release page. Treat it as an experiment, not a production recommendation.

## Action radar

### 1. Gemini CLI’s A2A server still leaves five routes unauthenticated

Gemini CLI 0.71.1 hardened its **A2A server** (the HTTP endpoint that accepts tasks from other agents) with bearer-token checks, loopback `Host` and `Origin` checks, server-side workspace trust, and checkpoint-path confinement.

- **The gap:** an [open, unmerged follow-up PR](https://github.com/google-gemini/gemini-cli/pull/30449) states that five manually mounted routes—including task creation and command execution—remain reachable without credentials.

- **What to do:** do not treat `0.71.1` as a full remote-exposure fix. Keep the A2A server **loopback-only** until a stable release closes those routes. If you do not run it, no action is needed.

### 2. Strands Decider v2 scores bounded agent actions in one pass

The Strands team [released five Apache-2.0 decision models](https://strandsagents.com/blog/introducing-strands-decider-v2/) built on Gemma 4 (`E2B` to `31B`) that score a supplied list of actions in one forward pass instead of generating free text.

- **Best fit:** tool or model routing, argument checks, answer gating, and multi-agent handoffs where the allowed choices are already known.

- **Vendor numbers and limits:** Strands reports the `31B` model averages **82.1** across six benchmarks (vs. 70.0 for the older `26B-A4B`), with expected calibration error improving from 0.062 to 0.027 (meaning confidence scores align more closely with accuracy). Model sizes are not directly comparable, and Decider cannot reason beyond the options you supply.

- **When to try it:** benchmark the smallest suitable checkpoint locally against your own logged decisions, measure false approvals and latency, and keep a confidence fallback or human escalation before using it as a safety gate.

### 3. Claude Ship turns GitHub PR policy into an agent loop

Anthropic launched [Claude Ship](https://claude.com/resources/articles/introducing-claude-ship) in beta for GitHub-connected Team and Enterprise plans, combining Code Review, a **PR Steward** (handles CI failures, merge conflicts, and review feedback), and repository policy files (`Claude Approvals`).

- Inside Claude Code, `/ship` can push a branch, open a PR, review it, and iterate on feedback and checks.

- Not available on individual Pro or Max plans, nor for zero-data-retention, HIPAA, or customer-managed-key organizations.

- **Why it matters:** policy files and branch protection become part of your security boundary. Anthropic recommends starting in **observe-only mode**; for an enterprise trial, use one low-risk repo with human branch protection, spending caps, and review of policy changes.

## Business opportunities

### Agentic API-abuse readiness audit for small SaaS teams

- **Evidence:** the NCSC report shows familiar API, identity, and config flaws are now probed at machine speed, highlighting API challenges and account/token/session rate limits.

- **Hypothesis:** a read-only readiness audit could map a small SaaS app’s public endpoints, inspect auth and throttling in an **authorized staging environment**, and prioritize fixes for login, signup, search, report, and LLM endpoints.

- **Validation & risk:** interview five small SaaS teams and run one manually scoped staging audit. Requires written authorization and bounded traffic, and cannot guarantee security.

### Submission-compliance evidence packs for indie mobile teams

- **Evidence:** rapid attention on `app-store-compliance` shows recurring friction tracking regional App Store and Google Play rules and declarations.

- **Hypothesis:** a lightweight service could turn official Apple and Google policy updates into project-specific evidence checklists with primary source links and a mandatory human-verification gate.

- **Validation & risk:** build one evidence pack for a real indie app targeting China and the EU to test review-prep time savings. Must never present community summaries as legal advice or official store guidance.
