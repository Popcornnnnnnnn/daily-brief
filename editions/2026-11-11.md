## Today in 90 seconds

- **Google released Gemini 4 Carbon for paid developers and enterprises.** It targets long-horizon agent work, hard coding and cybersecurity. The introductory API price is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens through November 30; Google says the regular price will double after that. Its benchmark and security-performance numbers are vendor claims, not independent reproduction.
- **AI assistants are moving from chat into business and shared work surfaces.** OpenAI launched business profiles, engagement insights and optional Business Agents. Anthropic added collaborative Sheets and Whiteboards, made Claude Data generally available, and opened interactive in-chat connectors. Both launches are useful but have material eligibility, geography and product limits.
- **Git shipped urgent fixes for six vulnerabilities.** The set includes code execution during recursive clone, path traversal and memory-corruption cases. Git 2.56.1 and maintenance backports are available; tools and services that bundle Git also need patched builds.

## Tech & AI

### Gemini 4 Carbon: a slower, deeper tier for long-running agents

Google made **Gemini 4 Carbon** available on November 10 through the paid Gemini API, Gemini for business, AI Studio and Android Studio. It is also rolling out to Google AI Ultra users in Antigravity, the Gemini app and AI Mode. Google positions Argon as the default for most software and knowledge work, while Carbon spends more tokens on difficult multi-step coding, scientific research and extended verification.

The commercial detail matters. Carbon has an introductory public API price of **$2 / million input tokens and $10 / million output tokens**. Google says the price becomes $4 and $20 after November 30. Antigravity is also previewing a **/swarm** mode that coordinates multiple agents in isolated workspaces with adversarial verifiers.

Google reports first-place results on CursorBench 4.0, Perplexity WANDR and Zapier AutomationBench at more than 2× lower cost per task than the next external model. It also reports strong vulnerability-finding, exploitation and patching results. Those are useful test leads, not neutral proof: several figures come from Google or selected launch partners, and the specialized Carbon for cyber access still runs through the Fairwind program.

[Google launch](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-carbon-now-available/) · [Developer overview](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/gemini-4-carbon-developers/)

### OpenAI gives businesses a profile, analytics and an agent inside ChatGPT

OpenAI launched **Businesses in ChatGPT** in beta. Eligible organizations can claim a profile through Business Hub, update core information, add product feeds, and see aggregated mention, click and discovery-topic data. OpenAI says individual conversations are not shared with businesses and that insights appear only above privacy thresholds.

Eligible US ecommerce and online-service businesses can also build a **Business Agent**. It appears as a clearly separate, labeled conversation from ChatGPT's independent answer. A company may sponsor the agent as an ad destination. This creates a new surface for product discovery and customer qualification, but it is not a universal rollout: agent eligibility starts with limited US categories, while profile claiming is broader.

[OpenAI announcement](https://openai.com/index/introducing-businesses-chatgpt/)

### AlphaProof Code formalizes a real Signal implementation

Google DeepMind introduced **AlphaProof Code**, an agentic formal-verification framework used with Signal engineers and external cryptographers. Human experts specified the properties and reviewed the proof strategy; Gemini agents generated more than six million lines of Lean, while humans reviewed roughly one percent, according to DeepMind.

The project proved security properties for key parts of Signal's production protocol implementation rather than only a simplified paper model. DeepMind also says the team reintroduced two previously fixed bugs and formally proved that the modified implementation was insecure. That is a valuable pattern: use agents to scale proof search, but keep specification, threat modeling and final judgment with experts. The framework is a research result, not a turnkey public auditing service.

[DeepMind announcement](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-alphaproof-code/)

### Claude expands into shared spreadsheets, whiteboards and live app surfaces

Anthropic released **Claude Sheets** and **Claude Whiteboards** in beta, made **Claude Data** generally available, and opened interactive connectors that can render partner apps beside the chat. The products are available across paid Claude plans. Claude Meetings was announced as “coming soon,” not released.

Sheets can collaborate on formulas, charts and multiple tabs, but the launch ceiling is 100,000 cells. It lacks pivot tables, protected ranges, multi-column sorting, macros, VBA, Power Query, DAX and live connected data; mobile is mostly read-only, and the beta does not support HIPAA, CMEK or ZDR. Those boundaries make it suitable for assisted analysis and lightweight operational artifacts, not a drop-in replacement for advanced Excel or governed data pipelines.

[Anthropic product update](https://claude.com/resources/articles/more-ways-to-work-with-claude-sheets-whiteboards-and-interactive-connectors) · [Claude Sheets limits](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/18324300-get-started-with-claude-sheets)

### Gemini Robotics 2.5 pushes the vision-language-action model toward partner deployment

Google DeepMind announced **Gemini Robotics 2.5**, its latest vision-language-action model for robots. It reports tests across more than 200 tasks, with 30–100% higher success and 20–30% faster execution than its prior system; a Mercedes-Benz and Apptronik proof of concept reported 96% pick-and-pack success.

These are developer-relevant signals for embodied agents, but availability is still partner-led rather than a broad public API. Hardware, safety validation and environment-specific training remain major deployment boundaries.

[DeepMind announcement](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-robotics-2-5-accelerates-real-world-physical-intelligence/)

## Open Source & Developer Tools

### OpenCode 2.2.7 fixes multi-client MCP sign-outs and changes SDK behavior

OpenCode is an open-source coding agent with terminal and desktop clients. Version 2.2.7 fixes a practical reliability problem: remote MCP servers that rotate refresh tokens should no longer sign users out when the TUI, desktop app, background service or several projects refresh the same login at once. It also prevents background compaction from stopping a running Claude Agent tool call.

**Try this if you use OpenCode with remote MCP or custom plugins.** First review two compatibility changes: `@opencode/ai` now exposes images through `response.images[0]`, and several SDK list/action endpoints return before plugins finish loading, so clients must refresh on matching update events. The release notes are project-authored; they document fixes and migrations, not independent reliability testing.

[OpenCode 2.2.7 release](https://github.com/anomalyco/opencode/releases/tag/v2.2.7)

### Copilot CLI preview adds CIDR sandbox rules and preserves ACP approval prompts

GitHub's **1.0.109-1 pre-release** adds IPv4 and IPv6 CIDR ranges to sandbox host allow/deny rules. It also changes ACP behavior so a pre-tool hook that returns `ask` delegates the decision to the ACP client instead of being treated as a host-policy denial. Together, these changes make network boundaries and interactive approvals more precise for teams that run Copilot CLI through an editor or another agent client.

**Do not treat this as a routine production upgrade yet.** If CIDR-scoped access or ACP hooks matter to your workflow, inspect the pre-release and test those exact policies in a disposable session; otherwise wait for a stable release. The notes also list environment-variable support in sandbox paths, but do not provide a security audit.

[Copilot CLI 1.0.109-1 pre-release](https://github.com/github/copilot-cli/releases/tag/v1.0.109-1)

### Catch-up: Ollama 0.40.16 cuts Apple-silicon memory use for Qwen3.8 Flash Next

Ollama's release, dated November 10 in Shanghai, says Qwen3.8 Flash Next uses about **27 GiB less memory on Apple silicon**. That figure is the project's claim and the notes do not publish a reproducible benchmark. The same release fixes `ollama launch claude` so Claude Code always routes to the selected local model, and changes the OpenAI-compatible `/v1/responses` endpoint to honor `truncation` instead of silently truncating by default.

This is worth a focused local test only if you use that model or route Claude Code through Ollama: compare peak memory and a representative long-context request before changing your default. This release landed just before the current 24-hour window and was not present in the previous seven briefs, so it is included as a catch-up item.

[Ollama 0.40.16 release](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/releases/tag/v0.40.16)

### Git fixes six vulnerabilities, including recursive-clone code execution

Git released **2.56.1** plus backports 2.55.1, 2.54.1, 2.53.1, 2.52.1, 2.51.3 and 2.50.2. The highest-impact issue, **CVE-2026-111760**, can turn a malicious recursive clone into code execution by abusing an integer truncation around extremely long index paths and replacing a submodule's `.git` file with a symlink.

The same coordinated release fixes protocol-v2 path traversal, stack corruption while stripping multiple signatures, out-of-bounds reads from expanded binary patches, heap corruption during very large merges, and unsafe filesystem access through fast-import features. GitHub has patched its hosted services and published GHES updates. Developers should update Git itself and check desktop clients, IDEs, CI images and other tools that bundle their own copy.

[GitHub security announcement](https://github.blog/open-source/git/git-security-vulnerabilities-announced-7/)

### Recent popular: GoogleChrome/modern-web-guidance

**Discovery date: November 10.** Chrome's Web Platform team published a preview repository that packages guidance for more than 250 modern browser features and 203 use cases as agent skills and a CLI. Its practical value is not another generic style guide: an agent can search by intent, retrieve feature-specific guidance, or scan a project for legacy patterns before it edits code.

The repository is stewarded with contributors from Chrome, Edge, Mozilla and MDN. A useful first test is the CLI's search, retrieval or legacy-scan flow on a small web project. The project is explicitly a preview; it helps an agent choose native APIs, accessibility patterns and fallbacks, but it does not replace testing across target browsers or product-specific compatibility policy.

[Repository](https://github.com/GoogleChrome/modern-web-guidance)

### Recent popular: storytold/craft-anything v0.6.0

**Release date: November 9; included now because the active release and recent community attention were not covered in the last seven briefs.** Craft Anything is a Rust clean-room reverse-engineering and recreation engine with a desktop app, CLI and MCP server. It exposes roughly 320 tools for web pages, native apps, games, firmware, packet captures and databases. Its build loop records evidence, runs comparisons and lets a coding agent refine the result.

Version 0.6.0 improved Ghidra-backed validation, macOS capture errors, process cleanup, approvals and packaging across many platforms. The low-friction entry point is `craft-anything-cli doctor` followed by a small, legally safe target. Limits matter: clean-room mode deliberately avoids internal code and files; deep analysis changes that boundary, builds need a separate coding agent, some adapters are less tested, and users remain responsible for authorization and intellectual-property constraints.

[Repository](https://github.com/storytold/craft-anything) · [v0.6.0 release](https://github.com/storytold/craft-anything/releases)

## Business Opportunities

### ChatGPT business profile setup and measurement for small companies

**Observed signal:** Business Hub gives companies a claimable profile, product feeds and aggregated discovery analytics; US businesses in eligible categories can add a Business Agent.

**Hypothesis:** Small ecommerce and service companies will need a lightweight service that verifies listings, normalizes product data, answers common buyer questions and measures how ChatGPT represents the business. A low-cost test is to recruit five eligible Shopify or service businesses and compare profile completeness, cited facts and qualified clicks before and after a structured cleanup. The main risks are limited geography and categories, evolving platform rules, privacy thresholds and dependence on one distribution channel.

[Source, November 10](https://openai.com/index/introducing-businesses-chatgpt/)

### Agent-aware web modernization checks

**Observed signal:** modern-web-guidance turns current platform capabilities and legacy-pattern detection into agent-callable skills.

**Hypothesis:** Teams adopting coding agents may pay for a narrow CI or review service that flags legacy browser patterns, proposes modern alternatives and records compatibility evidence. A small validation is a read-only scan of ten public or volunteered production codebases, followed by interviews on which findings were actionable. Risks include false positives, browser-support requirements that differ by product, and overlap with existing linters and consulting.

[Source, discovered November 10](https://github.com/GoogleChrome/modern-web-guidance)
