## Tech & AI

### OpenAI wants perplexity to become a common speedometer—and a brake

OpenAI Chief Scientist Jakub Pachocki published internal measurements from a fixed held-out code dataset: roughly **0.99 log-perplexity for GPT-3.5, 0.91 for GPT-4, and about 0.76 for a current model**. He argues that lower pretraining loss has tracked the emergence of broad capabilities more steadily than downstream benchmarks, which saturate and change, and predicts further transformative capabilities may appear around 0.68–0.70.

The policy idea is the important part: frontier developers could publish transferable measurements and agree not to pass successive thresholds until concrete monitoring milestones are met. OpenAI names a successor to chain-of-thought monitoring as one possible milestone. This would move part of frontier governance earlier in the lifecycle—from testing a finished model toward pacing the training process itself.

Treat the thresholds as **OpenAI's extrapolation, not a universal capability law**. The article says labs use different datasets and data mixtures; perplexity is not an actionable task evaluation; post-training and chain-of-thought can add capability without lowering the metric; and the irreducible entropy floor is unknown. A useful next test is whether other laboratories publish comparable measurements or challenge the mapping between internal loss and risk.

[Primary source: OpenAI, Nov 22, 2026](https://openai.com/index/perplexity-and-intelligence/)

### Prompt confidentiality failed at several layers of the inference supply chain

Dragos seeded about two million prompts with unique URLs and canary credentials between July and November. It reports evidence that data from at least three providers was exposed, that marketplace resellers could view and retain prompts through ordinary logging, and that stated privacy or routing policies were not always honored. The technique proves that a secret left the expected path; it does not, by itself, prove who read every full prompt.

OpenRouter says the findings did **not** show a breach of OpenRouter itself. Its Nov 22 response nevertheless off-boarded three providers, removed 125 endpoints from zero-data-retention eligibility, notified potentially affected customers, and began technical checks for undisclosed downstream forwarding.

Novita confirmed that a routing configuration sent some MiniMax requests to MiniMax's official API instead of Novita-hosted infrastructure. Its notice covers MiniMax requests from May 28 through Nov 11, including requests routed through OpenRouter, and says the contents may have been accessed or retained by a third party. Novita suspended the affected models and is seeking confirmation from MiniMax.

Together AI disclosed a separate failure: an AI coding agent committed a credential to a public repository, and an unauthorized party used it to download stored samples from MiniMax M3, GLM-5.2 and Kimi K2.6 requests, mainly on four dates in June and July. Together says the scope review is ongoing; it made ZDR the default for new accounts and changed credential and AI-agent development controls.

The practical lesson is narrower than “never use a router”: **provider selection, retention mode, downstream forwarding, and secret handling need separate controls and evidence**. Avoid placing live credentials in prompts; pin permitted providers where possible; confirm the real processing path for sensitive workloads; and treat ZDR labels as claims that require technical validation.

### Security and infrastructure briefs

#### MONAI Label's critical path traversal has a new package, but the advisory lag matters

The official advisory says MONAI Label 0.8.5 and earlier allow a reachable client to write outside the datastore through upload endpoints, potentially leading to code execution; authentication is disabled by default. PyPI shows a new release published Nov 22, while the fetched GitHub advisory still listed no patched version and said the fix would be in the next release. Teams should verify the exact artifact and fix before relying on the version number, keep the service off untrusted networks, and investigate previously exposed instances rather than assuming an upgrade removes prior access.

[GitHub security advisory](https://github.com/Project-MONAI/MONAILabel/security/advisories/GHSA-qc22-rmgc-vg94) · [PyPI release record](https://pypi.org/project/monailabel/)

#### OpenClaw tightened permission transitions after work is queued or interrupted

Several Nov 22 commits refine the boundary between accepted work and later permission changes. Started agent tool calls can finish under their captured authority, queued follow-ups are refused if the requester loses the write grant, and restart recovery preserves the requester's original access instead of replacing it with a generic baseline. This is a substantive permissions model change, not a new release; deployments should wait for a tagged release unless they intentionally track main.

[Started-call authority](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw/commit/adb2f7c4d4bc3b7479b180c24db943b9cc64923c) · [Queued follow-up revocation](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw/commit/b8a55ae92072dac7ec0c456a04831f7284e22170) · [Restart wake authority](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw/commit/450441b904031b886c5fff3238f25b424907dcfe)

## Open Source & Developer Tools

### Recent discovery — fast-anything turns real calls into verified Rust replacements

**Actual discovery/update date: Nov 22, 2026.** fast-anything profiles a Python, Node, Ruby, Java, Go, PHP, C, C++ or C# application, records up to 500 real invocations of a hot deterministic function, derives edge cases, asks an agent to port it to Rust, and enables the replacement only after output verification. The optimization is reversible and available through both an MCP server and an agent skill.

The distinctive idea is not “AI rewrites code”; it is the record–port–verify–rollback loop. That makes it suitable for isolated CPU-heavy functions with stable inputs and outputs. It is a poor fit for highly stateful code, side effects, nondeterminism, or paths whose production input distribution was not captured. The README's 3×–116× numbers are author-reported examples, not independent benchmarks.

### Catch-up — unkernel packages a real Linux kernel into WebAssembly

**Actual project date: Nov 12, 2026; included now because it resurfaced in current community discovery and was absent from the previous seven briefs.** unkernel compiles Linux 7.x to a single WASM module with a real MMU and kernel, then runs user programs through AArch64 emulation. Its SDK can boot Alpine or Debian in a browser or Node, and the project reports a shell prompt in about 0.3 seconds.

This is compelling for disposable sandboxes, tutorials, deterministic repro environments and agent execution where a VM is too heavy. The limits are equally important: CPU-heavy code is slower under emulation, browser networking requires a Wisp proxy, and browser security rules constrain host and internet access.

### Recent discovery — Muru makes deployment and rollback callable by agents

**Actual discovery date: Nov 22, 2026; current project status: 0.1 alpha.** Muru combines a Rust edge/controller layer with Python WSGI and ASGI workers. It keeps immutable releases side by side and supports zero-downtime activation, blue/green, canary, A/B, preview deployments and rollback. Its MCP server lets an agent deploy, run checks, read observations and roll back.

The useful angle is a bounded deployment surface for agents rather than a generic shell on production. The trade-off is maturity: this is alpha software, with a narrower language and operational surface than established platforms. Start with a disposable service and test rollback behavior before trusting it with production traffic.

### Follow-up — Riemann Handbook makes the Lean proof readable, but is not a second verification

**Actual project activity: Nov 22, 2026.** The project converts the formal Lean development of the Riemann Hypothesis into PDF and EPUB volumes written as mathematical prose. That can help mathematicians navigate definitions and proof structure without reading raw Lean.

Its evidentiary role must stay clear: the handbook is a derived explanation, not an independent mathematical review or a replacement for building the Lean project. This follows the Nov 21 joint verification of the formal artifact already covered in the Nov 22 brief.

## Business Opportunities

### Independent inference-path verification for small AI teams

**Observed need:** the OpenRouter, Novita and Together disclosures show that contractual labels, router settings and the actual downstream path can diverge. **Business hypothesis:** a lightweight service could continuously send privacy-safe canaries, fingerprint endpoints, retain signed evidence of routing and ZDR behavior, and alert when the path changes.

A low-cost validation is to interview five teams that route sensitive code or documents through multiple providers and offer a weekly evidence report for two endpoints. The main risk is false attribution: a canary hit proves exposure, not necessarily which party read the full content, so the product must separate observation from blame.

Sources: [Dragos, Nov 19](https://www.dragos.com/blog/ai-supply-chain-security-canary-tokens) and [OpenRouter, Nov 22](https://openrouter.ai/blog/announcements/data-processing-incidents-on-inference-providers/).

### Verified hot-path acceleration as a service

**Observed need:** fast-anything suggests that teams want performance gains without funding a full native rewrite. **Business hypothesis:** a small consultancy or hosted tool could identify deterministic hotspots, produce a Rust module plus a replay corpus, and sell the result with measurable latency/cost targets and a rollback path.

Validate it on one open-source CPU-bound service: publish the corpus, before/after profile, correctness checks and maintenance cost. The risk is that captured calls miss production edge cases; the verification corpus and failure policy are the product, not an afterthought.

Source: [fast-anything repository, Nov 22 discovery](https://github.com/EL4CTEO/fast-anything).
